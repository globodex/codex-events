# Community Event Retrospective

Read the pre-event baseline, current operating record, and authorized day-of and closeout records. Keep the planning timeline and source records intact. Identify when each source was captured and what it directly supports. Work with the evidence available. If important records are missing, produce a useful bounded retrospective and name the specific records that would resolve the gaps.

## Reconcile claims

- **Attendance.** Keep the final registration snapshot, prior forecasts, permitted capacity, unique checked-in registrants, walk-ins, volunteers, and any room count distinct. Deduplicate overlaps before combining counts. Explain missed scans or partial records and label sourced estimates. Calculate show rate only when the actual registrant attendance numerator and final eligible registration denominator are comparable.
- **Delivery.** Reconcile the latest run of show with a marked day-of copy, timestamped footage, operator notes or participant reports. Record which segments were delivered, changed, skipped or remain unknown. Record timing or whether a demo worked only when observed. Apply the same distinction to volunteer shifts, venue support, sponsor credits, hospitality and partner amplification. Invoices can verify payment. Verify raw media handoff, actual service and credit distribution directly when material.
- **Learning and reach.** Report the sample, collection window, respondent denominator where available, and permission status. Attribute individual comments or registration-source answers to their scope. Leave campaign conversion, broad satisfaction, later use and adoption unknown when the data is insufficient.
- **Costs and effort.** Use the latest receipt-backed reconciliation and its scope. Keep draft claims, reimbursements, shared equipment and open refunds visible where they affect the conclusion. Make a time-saved or ROI claim only if recorded effort and a credible comparison support it.

When a material claim is disputed, look for the most relevant primary record and explain remaining differences. Distinguish documented facts, attributed observations, estimates, and unknowns. Retain confidence and scope next to the claim. Mark the outcome unknown when its evidence is missing.

## Write the review

1. Lead with directly supported outcomes and their limits. For each material pre-event assumption, record the evidence, current status, and the smallest source or verification step that could close the gap. For attendance and program gaps, prioritize the actual check-in record and delivered run sheet or recording.
2. Separate workflows or templates another host can try from venue-specific choices and labor. Explain the evidence for each candidate, the necessary human owner or on-site checks, and what to measure in a small repeat. Link lessons to a specific friction or observation, such as stale facts across listings, unfilled staffing shifts, equipment research churn, or missing denominators. Keep proposed fixes testable.
3. Give a short, prioritized set of next-event decisions. Cover who will capture door counts, delivered slots, feedback and expenses, and when records should be closed out. Use an existing capture plan where one exists. Redact private details from external drafts. Confirm media permissions and publication approval before sharing externally.

If asked to update a particular review, preserve its provenance, useful history and access boundaries. Put the current findings up front, link the underlying evidence, and verify the saved review.
