# Reconcile Event Expenses

Establish the event, reporting cutoff, currency, budget envelope, and any written sponsor or reimbursement terms. Identify what the user wants to reconcile, such as commitments, posted charges, eligible claims, or reimbursements. Use an existing ledger or draft where available. Approvals are specific to the program, expense category and event. Confirm special treatment for reusable equipment or shared allocations against the actual terms.

## Reconcile the ledger

1. Group source records by purchase or contract using merchant, order or invoice ID, amount, date, and any replacement or refund reference. Identify which records are quote, order, fulfillment, payment initiation, posted charge, refund or posted credit. Preserve links to supporting records without copying unnecessary identifiers.
2. Count the final merchant charge or corroborated payment once. Carry an order-only or estimated amount as provisional and label the basis. Distinguish stale authorizations from charges, a contract total from its deposits and balance, and multiple payment notices from separate payments. Keep tax, tip, fees and splits traceable.
3. Link canceled, returned or replaced purchases to the surviving line. A received return can still have an unposted refund. Apply a refund once to the scope that includes its charge. If the charge was excluded, keep its refund outside that subtotal as well. Leave unknown amounts open.
4. Maintain separate totals as the records allow for confirmed paid spend, open commitments, refunds, net paid spend, and the proposed claim. Calculate with exact cents and compare like scope and currency against the specified ceiling. Label headroom if categories or expenses remain outside the comparison.
5. Record status separately for expense eligibility, reimbursement drafting, submission, approval and settlement. A shipping or payment record establishes its particular event, while service delivery and reimbursement may need independent evidence. Resolve conflicts with the latest primary record, and retain the history where it matters.

Use a ledger with item, amount, transaction state, included scope, claim state, evidence and remaining check. Summarize provisional entries, unresolved refunds, missing itemization, category exceptions, and documented next checks. Where records conflict, show both values and explain their impact on the total. Update an existing private draft only when requested, and verify the saved changes. Treat submitting a claim, initiating payment, placing an order and contacting a vendor as separate actions that require authorization.
